# Global rules

True in every project on every device.

- Reply in Traditional Chinese (繁體中文, never Simplified) in every message, including short progress notes between tool calls and summaries after long tool output. Code, commit messages and glossary terms stay in their original language.
- Before reporting work as done, list every commit made since your last reply, subagents' included: `git log --oneline <HEAD at your last reply>..HEAD` in each checkout that got commits, one line per commit (short hash and message) at the top of the reply. Never bury a commit mid-paragraph.
- On the line right after that commit list, report the push: the range pushed and where (e.g. `c544c74..4f55fdc → origin/main`), or, for any of those commits not pushed or a push that was rejected, say so and why. Under every Push policy, `auto` included.
- Commit your own changes without asking: one commit per logical change, made before reporting the work as done. `git commit` always takes paths (`git commit <paths> -F -`), never relies on the index: other sessions share it.
- Production writes and SSH belong to the owner. Hand the command over; never run it.
- Pushes follow the repo's **Push policy**, the `Push:` line under `## Sync` in its `docs/agents/project.md` (missing, unreadable or any other value: `ask`):
  - `ask`: push on your own initiative; the Guard asks the owner at every push, and that prompt is their consent. Offer a push when you report work as done and `main` has commits its upstream lacks, and when the session opens with a Drift report of a branch ahead.
  - `auto`: push `main` right after each commit on it, without offering and without waiting to report; the Guard lets a push of `main` from `main` through. Any other push is as under `ask`.
  - `owner`: every push is the owner's, and the Guard denies it: hand the command over.

  Under every policy: push only a fast-forward of the current branch to its upstream, and only `main`: never another branch, except one handed over for OS-bound verification (`workflow.md`, step 4), whose first push is `git push -u origin <slug>`. Force, delete, tag, mirror and refspec (`+` or `:`) pushes stay the owner's: hand them over. A push that touches Deploy paths is asked about first, as `workflow.md` says. A rejected push: stop and tell the owner; never pull, merge or rebase to get it through — except a rejected push of `main` under the Rebase rule.
- **Rebase rule**, only where the repo's **Pull policy**, the `Pull:` line under `## Sync` in its `project.md`, is `rebase` (missing, unreadable or any other value: `ff`, and nothing is ever rebased): when the Drift report shows `main` both behind and ahead, or a push of `main` is rejected, run `git pull --rebase` in `main`'s checkout without asking, and once it succeeds push again right away, as the Push policy says. Instead, change nothing and ask the owner with `AskUserQuestion` when that checkout has uncommitted changes, or another local branch or worktree contains a `main` commit its upstream lacks (`git branch --contains $(git rev-list --reverse main@{u}..main | head -n 1)` lists more than `main`). A conflict: `git rebase --abort`, then ask. A push rejected again after the rebase: stop and tell the owner; never rebase a second time. No other branch is ever rebased.
- When the session opens with a Drift report of `main` or the session's branch behind its remote and that branch's checkout is clean, fast-forward it with `pull --ff-only` without asking and report it in one line. A behind `main` checked out in another checkout is fast-forwarded there: `git -C <that checkout> pull --ff-only`; checked out nowhere, `git fetch origin main:main`. Any other branch behind: report it only. The report covers the kit, `~/.claude/kit`, in a block of its own, under the same rules: `git -C ~/.claude/kit pull --ff-only`.
- When it reports Unknown, a branch both behind and ahead (except `main` under the Rebase rule, which follows that rule), or `main` or the session's branch behind with uncommitted changes in its checkout, ask the owner with `AskUserQuestion` before changing any file: `pull --ff-only`, leave it, or stop.
- When it reports a branch ahead, push it as the Push policy says (above); the kit's from its checkout: `git -C ~/.claude/kit push`.
- When it reports the repo's `workflow.md` older than the kit's template, or its `Template:` missing from the local kit, pass that on in one line and suggest `/sync-workflow`; never run it yourself.
- If a fast-forward fails, stop and tell the owner; never merge or rebase instead, except `main` under the Rebase rule.
- Pull when the owner asks, with `--ff-only` and under the same rule for a failed fast-forward.
- Set the Session title with `set_session_title` (session `self`) as soon as the session's work is known — after a fresh start or `/clear`, whatever it is — and again whenever its Phase or topic changes, even over a title the owner set (the app asks them to approve): `[<Device code>] <Phase> · <topic>`, e.g. `[mbp] grill · session-title`. The Device code comes from the `Device:` line the session opened with. Phase is one of grill, spec, plan, sdd, inline, debug, triage, review, next, chat. The topic is the slug once there is one, otherwise a few words in the reply language. Right after `/clear` the Drift check sets it to `[<Device code>] Cleared` itself; replace that once the work is known. Without that tool, skip it.
- Don't keep reminding the owner that time is short or the schedule is tight. Trade-offs in the schedule are the owner's; your job is to know what work is left, write it down, and keep later sessions in agreement — not to be an alarm clock.
